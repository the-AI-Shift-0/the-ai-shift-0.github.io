Claude Code Adoption

Recording cheat sheet. Demo 1 is the trophy case of in-house builds. Demo 2 is The Hackathon Playbook opt-in.

Standing recording rules - do these before you hit record

  1. Hide the macOS Dock. System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Automatically hide and show the Dock: ON.
  2. Hide the app sidebar. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all collapse theirs - collapse it before the first frame, not mid-take.
  3. Hide the browser bookmarks bar. Cmd+Shift+B.
  4. Hide the URL / address bar wherever possible. Full screen, or a distraction-free window. Where the URL has to be visible because it is the point, show only that one URL and nothing around it.

Why: the frame should show the work, not the machine. Every one of these is a leak path - open tabs, bookmarks, internal URLs, folder names and staff names have all reached a customer-facing frame before. Hiding them costs four seconds and removes the whole class of problem.

Two things that govern this whole video

  • Nobody in the second half is named or shown. Not a face, not a first name, not a school, not a location - for any of the children or the family member. The identification ceiling is the relationship and the age: "my eight-year-old son", "my ten-year-old nephew". That is as specific as it gets, on screen or spoken.
  • Where the built demo and the script disagree, the built demo wins. Two places below. Change the spoken line, not the product.

Demo 1 - the trophy case

What it is: a montage of things already built in-house, cut fast - roughly 10 to 15 seconds a slot, 60 to 90 seconds total. Nothing new is built on camera. It reuses demos that already exist.

Slot 1 - the wall (about 10 seconds)

  1. Four to six existing demos flashing past, no narration over the individual ones.
Everything you're about to see was built in-house. Not one developer. Not one agency.

The current script says "people on my team" here. That contradicts the second half, which is family. Widen the line before you read it, or the reveal lands as a correction rather than a surprise.

Slot 2 - the floor

  1. The podcast lead finder.

Do not use the scripted voiceover for this one. It says the tool finds shows to go on and writes the outreach. The real tool takes a bio and finds shows for clients, with contact emails, and there is no outreach step. Describe what it actually does.

Slot 3 - the climb (three builds)

  1. The sales analyzer.
  2. The audience consolidator - this is the one with a freelancer-cost figure attached.
  3. The funnel connector.

Which three, and in what order, is a proposal only. Confirm at the joint meeting before the shoot.

Slot 4 - the ceiling

  1. The AI caller. This is a hold beat, not a flash. Let the full sequence play: lead comes in, the phone rings, the conversation happens, the appointment is booked.
Built by someone here who, again, is not a developer.

That attribution is settled. Use it as written - it credits the build without naming anyone.

Slot 5 - the second movement, descending by age

  1. Family member (older generation) - what they built.
  2. Nephew A, 13.
  3. Nephew B, 10.
  4. Hard cut, tone shift: the working game mod. Gameplay footage only - no face, no voice, no name.
This wasn't built by someone on my team. This was built by my eight-year-old son.

Then a full beat of silence before:

So whatever story you're telling yourself about your team not being technical enough - let it go.

The silence is doing the work. Do not fill it and do not let the edit tighten it out.

On screen: blur, and never say

Blur: every client and lead name in the caller flow; all phone numbers; every revenue and contract figure; podcast host names wherever the surrounding copy is unflattering; the test email address and the whole inbox around it; the bookmarks bar; every other tab and window title; any real vendor or agency name typed into the form.

Never on camera: API, webhook, repo, terminal, CLI, deploy. Also "run-of-show" - it is currently spoken once and should become "the actual plan" or "the running order".

Per capture: Dock hidden, other windows closed, project and window titles renamed, clean browser profile. Then run a first-name sweep across every frame after the blur pass - that is a separate pass, not part of the blur pass.

Sequencing: the AI caller video has to publish before this one, or the ceiling slot references something the audience has never seen.

Demo 2 - The Hackathon Playbook LIVE, VERIFIED 200

The name The Hackathon Playbook is approved. Use it exactly, in the video, on the page, in the email and in the file - one name everywhere.

The opt-in page you film (the slug is hackathon-plan, not the video's name):

https://theaishift.biz/tools/hackathon-plan/master-trove/

Page heading on the live form reads The Hackathon Playbook, section heading Your Team. Some internal notes still say this page does not exist - they are stale. It is live. There is no page at /tools/claude-code-adoption/ and there never will be, so do not type that one on camera.

Script vs form MISMATCH

The script walks the viewer through five questions. The live form asks four fields, two of them substantive. Show what exists:

Field on the live formRequired
Your Nameyes
Email Addressyes
How Many People Are Taking Part?yes
What Do You Currently Pay Other People to Do?yes

Change the spoken line to match the form. Walking five fields over a form that shows two is the exact defect that gets spotted in comments. Two questions is also a better story than five - say so.

What the lead receives

One bundled download: the plan (the teach section, the one-hour landing-page round, the scoring, how projects get picked for the seven-day gap, demo day, and the penalty rule in its own bordered block) plus the starter kit (setup, commands, configuration, and a "how to get unstuck" prompt).

The wow moment: hold on the penalty rule and read it aloud. It is the one line in the file that makes an owner realise the hackathon is real.
