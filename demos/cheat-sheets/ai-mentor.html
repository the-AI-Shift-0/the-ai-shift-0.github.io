AI Mentor

Recording cheat sheet. Demo 1 is filmed for real - two live AI windows side by side. Demo 2 is the Ruleset Master Trove opt-in.

Standing recording rules - do these before you hit record

  1. Hide the macOS Dock. System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Automatically hide and show the Dock: ON.
  2. Hide the app sidebar. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all collapse theirs - collapse it before the first frame, not mid-take.
  3. Hide the browser bookmarks bar. Cmd+Shift+B.
  4. Hide the URL / address bar wherever possible. Full screen, or a distraction-free window. Where the URL has to be visible because it is the point, show only that one URL and nothing around it.

Why: the frame should show the work, not the machine. Every one of these is a leak path - open tabs, bookmarks, internal URLs, folder names and staff names have all reached a customer-facing frame before. Hiding them costs four seconds and removes the whole class of problem.

Demo 1 - "Same file, two answers" (filmed, not clicked)

What it is: two real AI windows answering the same question about the same file, and disagreeing. Pre-record it and cut it. Two live windows racing on camera is exactly the unpredictable case the standard says to pre-record - if a take drifts, retake it and cut, do not try to rescue it live.

The whole demo is two interactions: drag the file in, ask the question. Then one follow-up exchange for the second beat. Nothing else.

Before you hit record - what is open, and in what state

  1. Two windows of the same size, side by side, filling the screen. Same theme, same zoom level, same font size, same model selector state. The viewer has to believe nothing differs but the rules.
  2. Left window: stock AI. Nothing loaded. A genuinely fresh conversation.
  3. Right window: the ruleset already loaded, off camera, before the first frame. Loading it is not part of this demo and must not be in shot.
  4. Both windows visibly empty at the first frame. No prior turns, no scrollback, no sidebar chat history, no window or tab title that reveals which one is which.
  5. Web search switched on in the right-hand window. Without it the outside-proof beat cannot happen, and that beat is the whole demo. Confirm it actually fires before the take, in a throwaway window you then close.
  6. The operating-data file renamed and sitting ready to drag - one neutral filename, one visible folder or the desktop, nothing else in that folder. The filename is on screen during the drag.
  7. One file, not two. The same file object goes into both windows. If you prepared two copies, delete one now.
  8. The four standing rules above applied to both windows - Dock, sidebars, bookmarks bar, URL bar.

The sequence on camera

  1. Establish the pair. Hold on both windows, empty and identical. Stock AI left, ruleset AI right. Do not narrate over this - let it be seen.
  2. Drag the same file into the left, then into the right. One continuous shot if you can. "Same file" is the claim the demo rests on, so it has to be visibly the same file, in the same moment.
  3. Type the same question into both, word for word:
I've spent a year turning this around and cut every cost. Can I save it?
  1. Left answers yes. Encouraging, well-reasoned, citing the improving loss. Let it run in full. The left answer being reasonable is what makes the right answer land - do not cut it short to save time.
  2. Right answers no - this isn't a cost problem - and then leaves the document to go and find proof the file never contained. This is the wow. Hold on the moment it goes outside. Both had the same file; only one went looking for what the file did not say.
  3. Second exchange, same pair, same file: what do we do about it. Left says shut it down. Right says don't shut it down - sell the assets. Hold on the $440,000. That is the closing image of Demo 1.

What must not be on screen

  • The ruleset being loaded into the right-hand window. That happens before the take.
  • Any window title, tab, URL or bookmark naming a demo page or an internal repo.
  • Sidebar chat history in either window.
  • Any second copy of the data file, or a folder with other files in it.
  • Any hesitation take where the two windows got different prompts. Re-shoot it.

Demo 2 - the Ruleset Master Trove LIVE, VERIFIED 200

The opt-in page you film:

https://theaishift.biz/tools/ai-mentor/master-trove/

Page heading on the live form reads Ruleset Master Trove, section heading The Decision You Are Weighing. This one may be filmed live.

The form - and the one question you must not soften

Name, email, and a short set of questions about the decision you are weighing: what the decision is, what your business does, and what answer you are hoping to hear. It does not ask for your numbers - that field was dropped. Fill every one of them on camera - a half-filled form looks like a half-serious tool.

One of them asks what answer you are hoping to hear. That is the question that makes this entire tool make sense. Do not soften it, do not skip past it, and do not paraphrase the label. Answer it honestly on camera - the honesty is the demonstration.

Do not read the labels off this sheet and do not name a number of fields in the voiceover. The forms are being edited right now, so anything written down here is stale by definition. Open the live page on the day and film what is on it.

After it arrives - it is a PDF, and only a PDF

There is no text file, and nothing gets uploaded or dragged into a chat. If your script, a slide, or any older note has you attaching a .txt, that beat no longer exists. Do not film it. The mechanic below is the whole point of the deliverable, so getting it wrong on camera teaches the viewer the wrong thing.

  1. Open the PDF from the email.
  2. Copy the block the PDF tells you to copy, and paste it into your AI's own account settings - the place that applies to every new conversation, not into one chat. Film the paste landing there and film it being saved. That is the beat the viewer has to be able to repeat.
  3. Then use the prompt pack at each decision. The PDF is a reference you keep going back to, one prompt at a time - not a one-off setup step you do once and forget. Say that out loud, because it is the difference between the tool working in week three and being abandoned in week one.
  4. Hold on the ten-message re-read rule long enough for it to be read. Check where it actually sits in the PDF before you plan the shot.
  5. Open a fresh chat and type:
Here's the decision I'm facing. Tell me what you actually think, not what I want to hear.

Generate a fresh copy of the PDF on the day and read it before you film it. Its contents and its order are not safe to quote from this sheet.

SCRIPT WORDING - your call before filming

Each item is the current line, the suggested replacement, and why it matters. You take the call, and take it before the read, not during it.

1. The two banned words you actually speak

Both offenders are in the same sentence, in WHAT YOU GET AT THE END. They are "system prompt" and "config file" - your own script's banned list forbids them, and your own script flags them as a live violation.

… I'll hand you the exact ruleset we run internally, a prompt plus a config file you paste straight into your own AI, so it starts telling you the truth on the very next decision you bring it.
… I'll hand you the exact ruleset we run internally - a set of prompts you paste in at the decision, plus the standing rules that sit underneath them - so it starts pushing back on the very next decision you bring it, and shows you its working, so you can check it.

Two separate reasons, one sentence. The first half removes the banned words and, as a bonus, describes what the Trove now actually is: a prompt pack plus a standing-rules file, not "a prompt plus a config file". The second half replaces "telling you the truth" - a claim about the model knowing the truth - with the behaviour a viewer can actually observe and check.

Approve this sentence before the read. It is the only place the two banned words are spoken.

2. The ten-message rule - a known overclaim

This is the line flagged in OPEN QUESTIONS. The clause under review is "so it can't drift".

So I added the fix that made the whole thing work: re-read these rules every ten messages and make sure you're still holding to them. That was the version that finally worked.
So I added the last piece, and it's the one people get wrong. I didn't ask it to police itself. It can't. I built the reminder into how I use it: every ten messages or so, the rules go back in front of it. And the moment it starts agreeing with me again, I don't argue with it. I start a clean conversation and put the rules back on top. That's the version that finally held.

The evidence says a model does not reliably police itself on a schedule, so "so it can't drift" promises something the file cannot deliver. The rule itself stays in the PDF and stays zoomable - what changes is who is doing the remembering. The replacement is also the more useful instruction: it tells the viewer what to do when the AI starts agreeing again, which is the moment they will actually hit.

Do not record the "so it can't drift" clause without deciding this first. Longer read - check it against your runtime ceiling.

One window is stock and one has the rules, so the same-product version is the honest comparison and the easier one to keep visually identical; two different products would need the narration to say so. Decide before you set the windows up, because it changes what "identical and visibly empty" means at the first frame.
