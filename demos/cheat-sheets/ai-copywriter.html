AI Copywriter

Recording cheat sheet. Demo 1 is your real copywriter project. Demo 2 is the AI Copywriter Master Trove opt-in.

Standing recording rules - do these before you hit record

  1. Hide the macOS Dock. System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Automatically hide and show the Dock: ON.
  2. Hide the app sidebar. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all collapse theirs - collapse it before the first frame, not mid-take.
  3. Hide the browser bookmarks bar. Cmd+Shift+B.
  4. Hide the URL / address bar wherever possible. Full screen, or a distraction-free window. Where the URL has to be visible because it is the point, show only that one URL and nothing around it.

Why: the frame should show the work, not the machine. Every one of these is a leak path - open tabs, bookmarks, internal URLs, folder names and staff names have all reached a customer-facing frame before. Hiding them costs four seconds and removes the whole class of problem.

Demo 1 - your real AI Copywriter, in ChatGPT

What it is: your genuine project, on screen, doing a real job. There is no built app and no simulation for this video - a simulated version was scrapped on purpose. What the viewer sees is the actual tool you use.

Length: 60-90 seconds, four beats, cut fast. Do not run it live on camera - record the generation, let the editor speed it up.

Before you hit record - the filename pass

  1. Open the project and read every filename in the list, twice. Client names, staff names, launch code names, internal project names - rename or remove. This list is the single highest-risk surface in the video.
  2. Rename the project itself. Its current name has never been documented and is assumed unsafe.
  3. Clean browser profile: no extensions, no other tabs, notifications off.

Beat 1 - the shelf (about 15 seconds)

  1. Open the project.
  2. One slow, continuous scroll down the file list. Do not stop and start - one move. The viewer should feel the depth, not read the names.
This is the project. Everything that ever made us money is in here - years of our own winning copy, the customer's exact language, and the best copywriters alive. All loaded in before I type a single word.

Beat 2 - one sentence (about 10 seconds)

  1. Type the prompt live and in full - do not paste. The typing is the point.
  2. Press Enter.
Write the full launch campaign for [product] - sales page, five-email sequence, three ad variations.

Replace [product] with your real offer. Everything else is verbatim.

Watch what one sentence does.

Beat 3 - the pour (about 25 seconds)

  1. Let the output run: sales page, then five emails, then the ads. Scroll with it.
A full sales page. Then five emails. Then the ads. In my voice, using my customers' words - because it already knows all of it.

Beat 4 - the hold (about 15 seconds)

  1. Stop scrolling and hold on one finished piece, long enough to read. Do not keep moving - the pause is what sells it.
Read that. That's not generic AI copy. That's better than anything my team ever handed me - and it wrote the whole campaign while I sat here watching.

Capture a second full generation

Run the whole thing again and record it. It costs two minutes now and it has saved a re-shoot before.

On screen: blur, show, never say

BlurShow sharp
Any revenue, spend or salary figure visible in the frame; client names; staff names; real customer names inside quoted emails; in Demo 2, the test email address, your own name in the form, and every other subject line and sender in the inbox. The file list (as a blur of depth, not readable names), the campaign structure, the generated copy itself, the form labels, the file contents, the prompt pack.

Never say on camera: project knowledge, corpus, context window, fine-tune, RAG, token, prompt engineering.

Demo 2 - the AI Copywriter Master Trove LIVE, VERIFIED 200

The opt-in page you film:

https://theaishift.biz/tools/ai-copywriter/master-trove/

Page heading on the live form reads AI Copywriter Master Trove, section heading Your Business Profile.

The form - what to do

Name, email, then a run of questions about your business - what you sell, what it costs, who buys it, the problem they arrive with, how your voice sounds. Some are optional. It is a longer form than the other videos use, and that is deliberate: this tool needs the detail to be worth anything.

The form no longer asks which campaign it should write first. That dropdown has been removed. If your script still has you choosing "sales page" or "email sequence" on camera, cut that beat and cut the line that goes with it - it points at a field the viewer will not see, and the form is in shot while you say it.

Fill it as a real owner would, and do not leave the optional fields blank on camera - a half-filled form looks like a half-serious tool. This is the one place in the video where filling in every box is the story.

Do not read the labels off this sheet and do not name a number of fields in the voiceover. The forms are being edited right now, so anything written down here is stale by definition. Open the live page on the day and film what is on it.

Shot order

  1. The form, filled and submitted.
  2. The inbox, the email arriving.
  3. Open the delivered file and scroll slowly: role instructions, craft layer, campaign blueprints, then hold on the customer-language section - that is the wow.
  4. A fresh project with the file added to it.
  5. The prompt pack, the iteration lines, the how-to-ask primer.

No live generation in Demo 2. The deliverable is the story here, not another output.

The delivered file is being edited right now. Generate a fresh copy on the day and read it before you plan the scroll - which sections it has, and what order they are in, is not safe to take from this sheet.

The line for the viewer to paste after loading the file:

Write me a launch campaign for my offer. Ask me anything you need first.

OPEN QUESTIONS - answer before filming

  1. What is the project currently called, and what will you rename it to? Its name goes on screen and has never been recorded anywhere.
  2. ChatGPT or Claude for Demo 1? The docs still disagree. Pick one, because the whole demo is built around that interface.
  3. Which claims are you willing to say on camera, and where is the evidence? The spend figure, the attributed-revenue figure, the recurring-revenue figure and the "a quarter of copy in 90 minutes" line all have no recorded source yet. Anything without evidence gets cut, not softened.
  4. Is the framing platform-agnostic? One assistant is currently never mentioned at all. Deliberate or an oversight?
  5. Which real offer goes in [product]? Decide now so the generated campaign is worth holding on screen.
