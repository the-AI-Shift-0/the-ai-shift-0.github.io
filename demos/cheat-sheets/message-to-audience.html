Message to Audience

Recording cheat sheet. Demo 1 is the built four-screen walkthrough. Demo 2 is the Research Engine Master Trove opt-in.

Standing recording rules - do these before you hit record

  1. Hide the macOS Dock. System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Automatically hide and show the Dock: ON.
  2. Hide the app sidebar. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all collapse theirs - collapse it before the first frame, not mid-take.
  3. Hide the browser bookmarks bar. Cmd+Shift+B.
  4. Hide the URL / address bar wherever possible. Full screen, or a distraction-free window. Where the URL has to be visible because it is the point, show only that one URL and nothing around it.

Why: the frame should show the work, not the machine. Every one of these is a leak path - open tabs, bookmarks, internal URLs, folder names and staff names have all reached a customer-facing frame before. Hiding them costs four seconds and removes the whole class of problem.

Settled decisions - do not reopen these on the day

  • The show is decided: The Amy Porterfield Show, hosted by Amy Porterfield. It is named on screen and nothing is blurred. The show name, the host name and the URL all render sharp. Blurring them was considered and rejected.
  • Demo 2 is copy-paste, not a link. Settled 2026-08-11: the assistant cannot fetch the page itself, so the lead selects all, copies, and pastes. Demo 1 takes a link. Demo 2 takes a paste. These are different mechanics and the script must not blur them together.

Banned outright: any download, listener or audience-size figure. No such number exists publicly for this show. It must not go on screen, it must not be spoken, and it must not be estimated out loud. If a number for audience size appears anywhere in a cut, that is a defect, not a rounding question.

Also absent, not blurred: reviewer handles. A blurred handle means the build put it there - a visible or blurred handle is a build defect either way. The quotes carry neutral labels only.

Demo 1 - "One link is all it needs"

What it is: a built four-screen walkthrough shown as the real system. Chain: index → l2 → l3 → l4. All four screens are required - the plan at the end is the payoff and the demo does not work without it.

https://the-ai-shift-0.github.io/demos/message-to-audience/

Unlock the demos first (sign in at /demos/) or the pages will bounce you to the home page mid-take.

Screen 1 - the link goes in

The built page shows the header "Research Engine - Appearance library loaded", the line "One link is all it needs", and the question "Which room do you want to research?".

The link is pre-rendered in the field - you do not have to type it. If you want a typing shot, this is the exact link, character for character:

https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-amy-porterfield-show/id594703545

The exact line to say over this screen:

It's learned from every room I've ever won.
  1. Click the field, or click "Research this room".
  2. Let the seven-step overlay run (about 2.3 seconds). It advances by itself - do not click through it.

Screen 2 - what it found

  1. Show the identity block: the show name, the host name, 885 episodes, since 2013, weekly, 4.9 from 4,575 ratings, and the volume of reviews and comments read.
  2. Pass over the six lens panels and the six verbatim audience quotes.
  3. Click "Compare to my best rooms".
It looks at six things, the same six I used to work out by hand.

Screen 3 - the comparison (the wow)

  1. Show the appearance counts: how many rooms, how many comparable, how many landed, how many died.
  2. Hold on the losing opener line - the one that says an opener was used in three comparable rooms and died in all three. That single line is the wow moment of the whole demo. Do not talk over it.
  3. Click "Build the running order".
It compares this room to the best rooms I've ever had, and tells me what worked and what fell flat.

Screen 4 - the plan

  1. Show the four time blocks: the open, the story picks, the turn, the ask.
  2. Then show the two closing panels: what not to use in this room, and the audience's own language.
It hands me back the plan for those 45 minutes: the open, the story, the turn, the ask.
It pulls the actual words that audience uses, not my guess at their words, theirs.

On the show itself, the agreed line is:

I'm in talks with them about going on.

Say exactly that. Anything stronger claims an appearance that has not happened, and anything vaguer makes the demo look hypothetical.

The six fields that actually exist - all required

  1. Your Name
  2. Email Address
  3. What Do You Sell?
  4. Who Do You Want in the Room?
  5. What Do You Want Them to Do Afterwards?
  6. Which AI Tool Do You Mainly Use? (dropdown)

The mechanic - say this carefully, it is the part that gets confused

Demo 1 took a link. Demo 2 does not. The lead opens the show's page, selects all, copies, and pastes into their project. Show it happening:

  1. Open the show's page in the browser.
  2. Cmd+A, then Cmd+C, on camera. The select-all highlight has to be visible - that flash of blue is what makes the instruction land.
  3. Paste into the project.
  4. Then type this, word for word:
I'm speaking on [name of the podcast or event] in two weeks. Run the audience research.

The select-all-and-copy instruction must be visible on screen and must be inside the delivered file's sourcing prompt. If it is only in one of the two places, the lead gets stuck at the first step.
