Sai's Guide - AI Copywriter

AI Copywriter - Demo 1

Record-day guide - keep this open while you film. Follow it top to bottom.

What you are filming

Your real ChatGPT project. The one you actually use. Not a built demo, not a mock-up, not a rebuilt version for camera. You open the real thing, scroll the real file list, type one real line, and let it run.

The only thing added is a light brand frame - a thin branded strip reading "AI Copywriter" laid over the real screen in the edit. Nothing on screen is invented.

Target length on the final cut: 60-90 seconds. Four beats, 65 seconds of them, fast cuts. This is not a tutorial.

Do this before the camera rolls

The filename pass is mandatory and it comes first. The file list is the highest-risk surface in this entire video - it is also the beat we are selling. Jump to Part 1 below and do it before anything else. Do not film first and clean up later; there is no fixing a leaked client name in the edit without losing the scroll shot.

Part 1 Mandatory pre-record filename pass
Highest-risk item on the shoot

Why this exists

From the script's own spec: "The file list is the highest-risk surface in this video. Filenames leak client names, launch names and internal project names."

Beat 1 is a slow scroll down that list, on screen, held long enough for the viewer to read it. Every filename in that scroll is published. This is the same defect class as AI CALLER TEST being readable on screen in an earlier video.

The checklist

Open the project's file list and go through it top to bottom

Rename or remove any file whose name contains any of the following. Do this in the project itself, before the screen recording starts.

  • A client name. Any customer, account or company we have worked with. Rename to the category instead, e.g. the type of business rather than the business.
  • A staff name. Anyone on the team, past or present - including the three copywriters. First names alone still count.
  • A launch name. Any product launch, campaign codename or offer name that is not public.
  • An internal project name. Anything only we would understand, anything with "test" or "v2" or a person's initials in it.
Sweep twice. A "First Last" pass misses single names. After the first pass, read the list again looking only for capitalised words you do not recognise as generic.
Also before you record

The project name and the sidebar

  • Rename the project itself if its current name leaks anything. The name shows in the sidebar and in the browser tab title for the whole shot.
  • Check the sidebar. It lists every other project on the account. Clear, collapse or hide anything that is not this one.
  • Check the window title and the tab title before you hit record, not after.
Part 2 Screen-recording hygiene
Clean frame

Set the screen up before the first take

  • Hide the macOS Dock. System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Automatically hide and show the Dock. An earlier video shipped with the full Dock visible - do not repeat it.
  • Close every other browser tab. Tab titles are readable at full screen.
  • Check window titles - the browser window title, the tab title, and anything visible in a second window behind it.
  • No bookmarks bar, no extensions, no notifications. Use a clean browser profile and turn on Do Not Disturb.
Part 3 The four beats, in order
Transition in

The line that gets you out of the story and onto the screen

Say this
"Let me show you the actual thing I use. Not a demo version - the real one I ran this morning."
15 sec
Beat 1

What's Loaded

Voiceover, word for word
"This is the project. Everything that ever made us money is in here - years of our own winning copy, the customer's exact language, and the best copywriters alive. All loaded in before I type a single word."
Editor direction from the script
Open on the project. Zoom slowly down the file list - folders stacked: winning VSLs, email campaigns, ads that converted, customer language, the copy legends. Dozens of files. The impact is the SCROLL - make the file list feel bottomless.
Scroll top to bottom in one move, no cuts mid-scroll. The beat is the length of the list, not any single filename.
10 sec
Beat 2

One Line In

Type this on camera, in full, word for word. Then hit enter.

Write the full launch campaign for [product] - sales page, five-email sequence, three ad variations.
Voiceover, word for word
"Watch what one sentence does."
Replace [product] with the real offer you are launching. Everything else stays exactly as written - the line has to promise a sales page, five emails and three ads, because that is what Beat 3 shows arriving.
25 sec
Beat 3

It Detonates

Voiceover, word for word
"A full sales page. Then five emails. Then the ads. In my voice, using my customers' words - because it already knows all of it. This took nine seconds."
Editor direction from the script
Copy pours out - sales page → email sequence → ads. Speed-ramp the generation. Cut fast; don't hold long enough to read. The wow is watching a full campaign build itself from one line.
Capture the generation at real speed. It will be slow on camera - that is expected and fine. The editor ramps it forward so it reads as instant. Do not try to make it fast in the room.
15 sec
Beat 4

The Proof

Voiceover, word for word
"Read that. That's not generic AI copy. That's better than anything my $25K-a-month team ever handed me - and it wrote the whole campaign while I sat here watching."
Editor direction from the script
Land on one finished piece - a section of the sales page or a single email - held just long enough to register it's genuinely good, not slop.

Part 4 The light brand frame - what it is and what it is not
For the editor

The frame is a strip, nothing more

The whole of Demo 1 is the real screen recording. The brand frame sits over that footage and never replaces any part of it.

  • What it is: a thin branded lower-third, or a thin branded header strip along the top edge, reading "AI Copywriter". Channel colours and channel type. It sits over the real project footage for the duration of the demo, or fades in at the start of Beat 1 and out at the end of Beat 4.
  • Keep it thin. It must not cover the file list during the Beat 1 scroll, the input box during Beat 2, or the copy during Beats 3 and 4. If it obscures any of those, it is too big.
  • NO invented UI. No fake buttons, no fake panels, no fake sidebar, no rebuilt version of the screen.
  • NO fake data. Every filename, every file count and every word of generated copy on screen is the real thing from the real project.
  • NO simulated chrome. No mocked-up app window, no invented product interface, no overlaid counters or progress bars that the real screen did not show.
A built simulation of this workspace was previously produced and has been scrapped. Demo 1 is the real project, filmed. Nothing on screen may be invented.

Part 7 Filming tips
Keep in mind

Four things that decide whether this is one take or three

Never film it live

Generation is slow on camera and a weak generation kills the beat. Record it, then let the editor speed-ramp it.

Run it more than once

The same prompt does not produce the same answer twice. Capture a second full generation so the editor has a choice if the first reads weak.

The scroll is the sell

Beat 1 works only if the list feels bottomless. Slow, continuous, no cuts mid-scroll.

Fast cuts, not a tutorial

Each beat is 10-25 seconds. Nothing is explained. The wow is volume, then speed.
