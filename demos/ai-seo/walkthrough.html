Two screens. The opt-in, then the two files and what you do with them. Rebuilt 2026-08-19 to the corrected product shape. Not a real audit.

Screen 1 - the opt-in → Screen 2 - the files and the AI →

What it shows

They fill a short form. Two files land in their inbox: a TXT that knows AI search inside out, and a PDF that tells them how to use it.

They load the TXT into their own AI, ask about their own company, and it tells them exactly what to fix.

The payoff is not on our page. It is inside their AI. Screen 2 says that in words, and beat 2 is drawn as their tool, not ours - grey chrome, its own file rail, its own message box.

What this demo is NOT

It is not a website that computes an audit for them. The previous build showed an on-page result map and that was the wrong product. If a slide, an older note or a script line has you filling a form and reading the answer off our site, that beat no longer exists. Do not film it.

Screen flow - seven beats, three clicks on screen 2

  1. Open index.html. First field already focused. Nothing moves on its own.
  2. Fill the fields, click one AI chip, pick Small businesses from the dropdown.
  3. Click Send me the two files. Five steps tick through, about three seconds.
  4. Screen 2 opens on the two files. Pause here. The filenames are the beat.
  5. Click Add file to this project. The TXT appears in the file rail, marked in project knowledge.
  6. Click Send. The question goes up and the AI answers - it refuses to guess, and hands over six questions to run.
  7. Click Send again. The pasted answers go up, and the three fixes come back.

An empty field on screen 1 nudges instead of advancing, so an early click cannot skip the sequence. On screen 2 nothing is on a timer - every click is yours.

The wow moment

The AI refuses to guess. A sceptical owner expects a confident invented answer. Instead it says it has not seen anything, will not guess, and hands over the work. Then the fixes arrive built only out of what was pasted back.

Second-best moment: 2 of 9 on the unnamed questions. Named, they describe us fine. Unnamed, we are barely there. That is the whole problem in one line.
